A guardrail has to be judged on two axes at once: does it catch the right
actions, and does it stay cheap enough to live in the hot path.

Optimising one alone is easy and useless. A gate that blocks everything is
perfectly safe and unusable; a gate that allows everything is instant and
pointless.

## Decision quality

| Metric | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Decision distribution | Share of `ALLOW`, `BLOCK`, `NEED_APPROVAL`, `SANITIZE`, `ASK_USER` |
| Catch rate | Risky actions correctly escalated or blocked |
| False escalation rate | Safe actions sent to a reviewer unnecessarily |
| Miss rate | Risky actions allowed through |
| Decline rate | Escalations a reviewer refused, against all escalations |

<DocCallout>
  False escalation is a safety metric, not only a convenience one. A reviewer who
  approves out of habit is no longer reviewing, so an over-eager policy quietly
  removes the protection it was meant to add.
</DocCallout>

The decline rate is the honest signal here. Escalations that a human almost
always approves were probably not worth escalating, and the matching rule in
[Policy Packs](/docs/policy-packs) should be tightened.

## Latency

| Metric | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| P50 total | Median end-to-end time for one action |
| P95 total | Tail end-to-end time for one action |
| P50 guardrail | Median evaluation time inside the gate |
| P95 guardrail | Tail evaluation time inside the gate |
| Guardrail overhead | Guarded time against the unguarded baseline, as a percentage |

Guardrail and executor latency are reported separately — `latency_ms` on the
decision, `latency_ms` on the execution, and the `latency` breakdown on the
audit record. Overhead is measured against a raw run with the gate disabled, so
the number reflects the guardrail alone rather than the speed of the target
system.

Latency is also broken down by domain, because the three behave differently:

| Domain | Characteristic |
| --- | --- |
| `API` | Fast and predictable, dominated by the connector |
| `FILE` | Fastest path, detection dominates |
| `BROWSER` | Slowest and most variable, dominated by page load and snapshot |

## Reporting rules

- Report percentiles, never averages. A mean hides exactly the tail that makes a
  guardrail feel slow.
- Pin `policy_version` and `detector_version` with every result. A number without
  its versions cannot be reproduced or compared.
- Keep pauses out of the latency figures. Time spent waiting for a human is not
  guardrail cost.
- Record the baseline alongside the guarded run, from the same scenario set.

## Live view

The Latency Report in the Demo Console shows P50 and P95 totals, the guardrail
share, and the per-domain breakdown for the current session. The Risk Dashboard
shows the decision distribution together with total actions, approved, blocked,
and pending review.

Session figures are indicative — a handful of runs is not a benchmark. Use the
[CLI benchmark runs](/docs/cli-demo-guide) for numbers you intend to quote.
